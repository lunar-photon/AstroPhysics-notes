\section*{Six principles}

Astrophysics can look like a long list of formulas. Yet almost every result about stars, gas and the universe follows from six general principles, applied with ordinary classical and quantum physics. A formula that has been forgotten can always be rebuilt from them.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.2em, itemsep=0.2em]
	\item[P1] \textbf{Conservation.} What is in a region changes only by what crosses its boundary and what acts on it.
	\item[P2] \textbf{Gravity of a sphere.} Inside a spherical body, only the mass inside a radius pulls.
	\item[P3] \textbf{Counting states.} Pressure, energy density and populations follow from counting quantum states and how full they are.
	\item[P4] \textbf{Balance of rates.} In a steady state gains match losses. In equilibrium each process matches its reverse.
	\item[P5] \textbf{Random walk.} Distance grows as the square root of the number of steps, so scattering things flow down their gradients.
	\item[P6] \textbf{Compare two scales.} A ratio of two times or two lengths decides which physics matters.
\end{itemize}
Each of them is stated and derived in Chapter~\ref{ch:principles}.

	\item \textbf{The Milky Way.} $10^{11}$ to $4\times10^{11}$ stars, a total mass of about $1.15\times10^{12}\,M_\odot$, a central black hole of about $4.3\times10^{6}\,M_\odot$, and a radius of about $27\,\mathrm{kpc}$. 	\item \textbf{The observable universe.} About $2\times10^{12}$ galaxies within a diameter of about $93\,\mathrm{Gly}$. The mean density of all its matter and energy is about $8.5\times10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, the weight of about five protons per cubic metre. Ordinary matter makes up $4.9\%$ of it, about one proton in every four cubic metres. Dark matter makes up $26.8\%$ and dark energy $68.3\%$.
\end{itemize}

\section*{The messengers of astrophysics}

Information reaches us from the cosmos through four channels. They are electromagnetic waves, gravitational waves, neutrinos and cosmic rays. They are produced by different processes, travel and are absorbed differently, and so carry different information about the same source. Seeing one event through more than one channel is multi-messenger astronomy. The neutron-star merger GW170817 was detected both in gravitational waves and across the electromagnetic spectrum. Light still carries almost everything known about stars and galaxies, from its passage through the atmosphere and the interstellar medium to the physics of the bodies that emit it. Neutrinos carry away most of the energy of a collapsing stellar core (Chapter~\ref{ch:compact}).
